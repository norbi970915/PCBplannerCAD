The final rejected dense-board pair drag now succeeds. Connected shove can reshape the affected portion of an existing conductor around clearance contours while keeping its legal prefixes, tails and fixed endpoints. Both contour directions and hull orders are considered, with checked anchor-to-anchor search retained as a fallback. This lets a displacement propagate through several neighboring nets without rebuilding each complete route.

A fixed via endpoint was physically clear but fell 13 nm inside an extra-padded contour. Connected shove now omits that optional padding while retaining the required physical clearance and full DRC. Fixed vias and footprints remain unchanged. The default routing-search padding remains unchanged.

Temporary split documents now use isolated topology and spatial-index caches for their candidate searches. Their caches cannot leak into the enclosing source document, and all candidates still share the same total work allowance. Short paired bodies retain coupling; a bounded adjacent-bend attempt consumes old bends before creating another split. This prevents repeated moves from silently becoming single-net drags. Identity-preserving splits on arbitrary headings now use exact integer lattice points and cannot introduce a kink.
